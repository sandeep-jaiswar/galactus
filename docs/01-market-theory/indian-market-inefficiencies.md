# Galactus — Indian Market Inefficiencies

## Purpose of This Document

This document identifies **structural inefficiencies specific to Indian financial markets** that make capital-behavior inference both possible and durable.

These inefficiencies are not temporary mispricings.  
They are the result of market structure, regulation, participant mix, and incentives.

Galactus is explicitly designed to exploit understanding of these inefficiencies—**not to eliminate them**.

---

## Core Observation

Indian markets are not inefficient because participants are irrational.

They are inefficient because:
- Capital types are unevenly distributed
- Constraints are asymmetrically applied
- Information and execution are temporally misaligned

These conditions persist by design, not by accident.

---

## Inefficiency 1 — Extreme Retail Dominance by Count

Indian markets exhibit:
- A very high number of retail participants
- A much smaller number of institutions controlling capital

Consequences:
- Order count is dominated by retail
- Capital value is dominated by institutions
- Price often reflects **liquidity stress**, not conviction

Retail capital:
- Is reactive
- Is attention-driven
- Has limited ability to absorb adverse movement

This creates predictable **crowding and exhaustion patterns**.

---

## Inefficiency 2 — Derivatives Dominance Over Cash

In India:
- Index and stock derivatives volume often exceeds cash market volume
- Price discovery frequently occurs in the derivatives market
- Cash market follows derivative-implied constraints

Key implications:
- Options positioning can pin price near expiries
- Dealer hedging flows can dominate directional intent
- Price can move without corresponding delivery interest

Galactus treats derivatives as **structural drivers**, not speculative noise.

---

## Inefficiency 3 — Forced Capital From Expiry Mechanics

Weekly and monthly expiries create:
- Predictable time-based constraints
- Forced unwinds and rolls
- Concentrated hedging activity

Unlike discretionary trades, expiry-driven capital:
- Cannot be delayed
- Often ignores price attractiveness
- Operates under hard deadlines

This creates **repeating structural pressure windows**.

---

## Inefficiency 4 — Index Concentration and Rebalancing Effects

Major Indian indices:
- Are highly concentrated
- Experience periodic forced rebalancing
- Drive significant passive capital flows

Index inclusion, exclusion, and weight changes create:
- Non-opinionated buying and selling
- Predictable execution windows
- Liquidity distortions in constituent stocks

These flows are large relative to free float and daily liquidity.

---

## Inefficiency 5 — Disclosure and Information Latency

Although disclosures are public:
- Timing is discrete, not continuous
- Market digestion is uneven
- Reaction often precedes full interpretation

This creates windows where:
- Capital reacts to *mechanics* before *meaning*
- Price moves without consensus understanding

Galactus does not interpret disclosures narratively; it observes **capital response patterns**.

---

## Inefficiency 6 — Liquidity Fragmentation

Indian equities often exhibit:
- Sharp liquidity cliffs
- Wide variation in free float quality
- Limited depth outside index names

Consequences:
- Small forced flows can create large price impact
- Illiquidity amplifies structural pressure
- Volatility is often liquidity-driven, not informational

Galactus normalizes all inference by **liquidity and float awareness**.

---

## Inefficiency 7 — Behavioral Feedback Loops

Retail behavior in India is strongly influenced by:
- Recent price action
- Social media and messaging platforms
- Derivatives payoffs

This creates:
- Self-reinforcing attention loops
- Overreaction to short-term outcomes
- Late-stage participation in crowded moves

Galactus uses retail behavior only as a **crowding proxy**, never as truth.

---

## Why These Inefficiencies Persist

These inefficiencies persist because:
- Retail participation is encouraged
- Derivatives provide leverage without proportional education
- Passive capital continues to grow
- Market structure rewards activity over understanding

None of these are likely to change quickly.

---

## Implications for Galactus

Because of these inefficiencies:
- Capital constraints are more visible
- Forced flows are more impactful
- Structural inference is more reliable than prediction

Galactus is designed to:
- Observe these mechanics
- Quantify their pressure
- Avoid competing with narratives or speed

---

## Limits of Exploitation

Galactus does **not** assume:
- Inefficiencies always lead to profit
- Structural pressure always resolves directionally
- Retail behavior is always wrong

It assumes only that **constraints leave fingerprints**.

---

## Final Statement

**Indian markets reward those who understand structure, not those who chase movement.**

Galactus exists to model that structure.
